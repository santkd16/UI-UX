Plastic pollution

Single-use plastic, incorrect waste separation, and littering affect streets, rivers, and biodiversity. We must reduce waste and separate recyclable materials.

Since 2024, Colombia has been implementing rules to gradually phase out certain single-use plastics.

Local recyclers

Colombian recyclers are essential workers in the waste recovery system. Our campaign will encourage students to separate clean, dry materials and respect local collectors.

Waste recovered for use

Of ordinary municipal waste reported~20%

Approximate share of ordinary waste reported as recovered under Colombia's public cleaning-service system.

Colombia, Decree 772 (2026), citing 2024 SSPD data ↗

Important comparison note: These are different indicators and reporting scopes. Australia's figure includes commercial, industrial and construction streams plus some energy recovery; Colombia's figure concerns ordinary waste in the public cleaning-service system. Do not interpret the difference as a directly comparable recycling gap.

Live podcast · 3 speakers · English oral task

Our voice can make a difference. 🎙

Use this website like a television studio: introduce the countries, explain the numbers, compare organizations, and launch the campaign. The complete script below includes modal verbs, future forms, conditionals, and Past Perfect.

SPEAKER 1 · INTRODUCTION & AUSTRALIA
“Hello everyone! Welcome to our intercultural podcast, Recycle Today, Protect Tomorrow. Imagine we are in Australia. I am speaking to you from Canberra, the capital city. Australia has about 27.9 million people. English is the main language, and First Nations communities have their own languages. You can find famous places like the Sydney Opera House, and you should try meat pies or lamingtons! Australia uses technology such as bottle-return machines, but it still faces serious plastic pollution. We must protect our environment.”

SPEAKER 2 · COLOMBIA & STATISTICS
“Now welcome to Colombia! Our capital is Bogotá, and our country has about 53 million people. Spanish is our main language, and Colombia has approximately 68 native languages. People enjoy arepas and Colombian coffee. We have beautiful ecosystems, but plastic waste is a problem. Let's compare numbers carefully: Australia reported 67% resource recovery in 2024–25, including several waste types and energy recovery. In Colombia, about 20% of ordinary waste in the public cleaning-service system was reported as recovered in 2024. These figures do not measure exactly the same thing. If people separate waste, recycling becomes easier.”

SPEAKER 1 · ORGANIZATIONS & HISTORY
“Organizations can make a difference. In Australia, DCCEEW works on national policy, APCO works on sustainable packaging, and Clean Up Australia encourages communities. In Colombia, MinAmbiente, SuperServicios and local recyclers are important. Australia had developed recycling policies before the 2024 National Waste Policy Action Plan. Colombia had approved a law in 2022 before issuing its 2024 plastic regulation. Governments should cooperate with citizens, and schools can teach better habits.”

SPEAKER 3 · CAMPAIGN & SOLUTIONS
“Now we are going to present our proposal for Colombia: Recycle Today, Protect Tomorrow! Our slogan means that small actions today can help future generations. We will prepare posters, talk to students, promote reusable bottles, and invite local recyclers. If students participate, our campaign will reach more people. If every school recycled correctly, our cities would be cleaner. We are meeting as a team to organize the activities, and we will measure real results after the campaign. You should reduce unnecessary plastic, and you must respect local recyclers.”

ALL THREE · REFLECTION & ENDING
Speaker 1: “What had governments done before our proposal? They had already introduced waste and plastics policies.”
Speaker 2: “What can Colombia learn? Education, community programs and clear information are important.”
Speaker 3: “If we work together, we will make a positive change in our community. Recycle today, protect tomorrow!”
All: “Thank you for listening!”
